Trace Check-up · a neutral check-up for AI-agent logs

Check your agent's logs before you pay to train it.

A small tool that runs on your own computer. Give it the log your agent already writes. In about a minute: what trying again fixes, what training might add, and what the log is missing.

See an example report (on a made-up log; also in 中文). It is open source: the code is published when the first team asks for it.

From log to next step

1. Give it your log

The file your agent already writes. Nothing new to set up.

2. Checked in about a minute

Measured on your own log, not on someone else's log.

3. Train, or try again

Side by side, counted in tries. Or “can't tell yet”, and what is missing.

Training costs money. Trying again costs money too.

Pick on a hunch and you may pay for the wrong one. Check your log first.

What it answers

  • What to record. The eleven things a useful log records, each marked recorded, partly recorded or missing: every action and what came back, a task name, whether each conversation worked and who decided that, the tokens each one used, and more. The full list, as the report shows it on a made-up log: What your log records.
  • How much is enough. Each task tried 8 times, each marked worked or failed: then you can see how much trying again already fixes. And about 5,000 conversations that worked, each saying who decided it worked, before training. Below this, published results go both ways: training made some models better and some worse (MUA-RL; ToolRL). The line is a rough guide, not a measured minimum. Many different tasks tell you more than many copies of a few.
  • Train, or try again. Two cards side by side, counted in tries: what letting the agent try again does, measured on your log, and a rough range for training a small model, from published studies. Each card says how sure it is, what it rests on and what would change it. With too little to go on, it says “can't tell yet” and what to collect.

Running it yourself

Trace Check-up is open source: the code is published when the first team asks for it: write to shuhuiq@stanford.edu. Until then, the example report shows what it makes: the page you would get on your own log, here on a made-up one.

It reads OpenAI chat, OpenTelemetry GenAI, Arize Phoenix, LangSmith, Langfuse, Claude Code, ShareGPT, Microsoft ToolTalk, CSV / Excel and JSON lines. A log it cannot read gets no report, and it says why.

Private by design

  • It runs on your computer. Nothing is uploaded to us or to anyone else.
  • It makes no network or API call, and runs on the CPU.
  • It reads only the file you give it, and never changes it.

Neutral by design

  • No training platform pays us, so the advice doesn't depend on which way you go. It includes “don't train”.
  • Every sentence points to its source: a reading from your own log, or a published result named with its paper. When there isn't enough to go on, the report says it can't tell.
  • We don't promise that training will help.

After the report

  • Train it yourself, on any platform you like. A log of conversations and tool calls is enough to train by copying the examples that worked; training by trial and reward also needs a way to score each try. The report shows which conversations worked and which had problems. Not built yet: a list of the ones to train on.
  • Compare after training (not built yet): give it the trained agent's new log as well, and it compares it with your untrained agent trying again at the same cost, on the same tasks. With too few tasks, it says it can't tell.
  • Or don't train. The advice is the same whichever way you go.

Questions

What does “at the same cost” mean? Letting the agent try a task again until it works takes more tries. The report counts on your log how many tries that takes on average, and weighs training against it: one try against that many. If your log records tokens, it also says how many tokens one try uses.

When is trying again not an option? When the agent can't tell, as it runs, that a try failed, or when its actions change records and aren't safe to do twice, such as a refund paid twice. The report asks you about both.

How sure is the forecast? Each card gives a direction and a range, not a single number. Each card says how sure it is, what it rests on and what would change it. With too little to go on, it says it can't tell.

What does my log need to have? Each conversation, every action the agent took and what came back, and ideally whether the task worked and who decided that. The report lists what is missing.

Who can see my log? No one. It is read on your computer and not sent anywhere.

Not built yet: comparing a trained agent's log with the untrained one, a list of conversations to train on, and counting who decided a success in a table export. It trains nothing. Its checks were built on one project's own logs; yours will break assumptions those did not.
