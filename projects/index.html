Three products for teams that put AI agents to work: check what the agent does, pay less for the answers, and know whether training it is worth the money.

MORPHEUS.ai · for teams that let AI agents plan and act

Know which of your requirements an agent breaks — before it acts.

MORPHEUS checks every step an AI agent plans or takes against the requirements you gave it, and tells you, for each requirement, whether it is met, broken, or cannot be checked — with the evidence.

  • Check. Your requirements are kept across the whole conversation, and a verdict rests only on what code can confirm — the plan's own “total: within budget” never counts. When it cannot tell, it says so instead of guessing. On a blind test it let none of 8 planted budget overruns through (2 flagged as broken, 6 as cannot-check); the LLM judges let 2 to 8 of them through.
  • Stop. Inside Claude Code, Codex and Cursor it blocks the tool call that would break a rule you set — a new dependency, a force push, an edit to the file that computes your metric — and checks the agent's “done” before you believe it. Replayed on 4,750 real tool calls: 10 blocks, all correct.
  • Fix. When a step breaks a requirement, it proposes alternatives at that step and re-checks them before you see them.
  • Record. For ML experiment loops, it turns a run into a record anyone can re-verify: a frozen evaluator, the noise across seeds, and an audit for metric gaming.

Where it came from: a planning engine for science (Morphism-Oriented Reasoning with Provable, Honest, Epistemic Understanding for Science) that builds a knowledge graph from papers — 18,600+ actions from 665 documents, every one traced to its page — plans over it, and accepts a plan only after a verifier passes it. Research: EMNLP Findings 2025 and four 2026 preprints. Write-ups: what MORPHEUS is and does · the judge that agrees most is not the safest · related work: judges, verifiers and guardrails.

Rung · for teams paying for LLM calls

Pay for the expensive model only when the cheap one is shown wrong.

One OpenAI-compatible model name. Behind it, Rung tries the cheapest capable model, checks the answer, and escalates only on a verified failure, under a hard budget per task. Every response says who answered, what each attempt cost, and whether anything checked it.

  • No prediction. Guessing the right model from the prompt recovered none of the available savings in our tests; climbing on evidence did. Ahead of every single model in 37 of 39 matched-spend comparisons on HumanEval+.
  • A check that needs nothing from you. If you have tests, Rung uses them. If not, it asks the same model three times and requires it to agree with itself. 0.78–0.98 correlation with correctness, at about 3 % of one top-model call; an LLM grader came out worse than chance.
  • Routing by kind of task. Your own traffic builds a table of cost per correct answer for each kind of task, so the expensive model is used where it actually wins. Its advantage was 58 points on one category and 2 on another.
  • Drop-in. Point your existing OpenAI client at it; nothing else changes.

Status: working and measured, not yet battle-tested. Write-up: Don't predict, verify · related work: routers and cascades.

Trace Check-up (trace2wm telemetry) · for teams running AI agents

Before you train your agent, find out if it's worth it.

It reads your agent's log on your own computer. In about a minute it says what the log is missing, whether there is enough of it, and how training compares with letting the agent try again, counted in tries — or that it can't tell yet, and what to collect.

  • Train, or try again? Two cards side by side: what trying again does, measured on your own log, and a rough range for training a small model, with the published results it is based on. Either card can say “can't tell yet”.
  • Do you have enough? Tasks tried at least 8 times, conversations that worked and who decided they worked, tasks that never worked — and how many more to collect.
  • What to record. The eleven things a log needs before it can answer, and the ones yours is missing.
  • Honest by construction. Every sentence points to a reading of your log or a cited paper, and it says “can't tell” when the log cannot decide. It never promises that training will help, and no training platform pays us, so the advice does not depend on which way you go.

Status: a small tool that runs on your own computer, so your log never leaves it. The code is not public yet; the example report (on a made-up log) shows what it makes. Comparing a trained agent with the untrained one at the same cost is not built yet. Product page.
