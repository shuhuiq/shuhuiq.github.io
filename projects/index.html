Morpheus · morpheusplan.com · invite-only beta

AI says “done.” On what grounds?

Evidence you can check, for every AI conclusion and every AI action. Write your requirements the way you would say them — “$600 in total at most”, “arrive in Boston by 12:00” — and give Morpheus the AI's plan or action. Each requirement gets one of three verdicts, and each verdict says who decided it: computed by code, judged by AI, or confirmed by you. The three are never mixed.

  • Met, with the evidence. The arithmetic, the quote or the run result behind it: “Line 1 arrives at 11:45, 15 minutes early.” Not “looks good”.
  • Violated: which line, by how much. “190 + 420 + 15 = 625 > 600. That is $25 over.” It is fixed in place, and only what changed is checked again.
  • Unclear: what is missing, and who supplies it. A fact is missing: fetch it, then check again. Several options fit: it lists them, and you pick. Nothing fits: re-read the requirement. “Found nothing wrong” is never counted as “met”, and it never forces an answer.
  • Same model, plus Morpheus. Trip planning, 296 unseen tasks: 56% → 98% solved, none of 282 “met” verdicts wrong, and no task broken by a fix; at 10 cities, 9 → 35 of 37. Group travel, 30 unseen tasks with 204 travelers: 4.4% → 84.8%, 1 wrong “met” in 1,086.
  • Also for AI agents. Coding agents: it reads each command before it runs, inside Claude Code, Codex and Cursor, and stops the ones that break a rule you set. Research agents: records of ML experiments that anyone can re-run: the scoring program is locked, and each change is run several times. Replayed on 4,743 real coding-agent commands: it stopped 10, all correctly, and nothing it should not have. Experiment records: of 12 fake improvements we planted, it accepted none; it kept all 3 real ones; an outside re-run agreed on 11 of 11.

Try it: morpheusplan.com. Invite-only beta, free during the beta; ask for an invite at info@morpheusplan.com. Trip planning: Natural Plan (arXiv 2406.04520), scored by the benchmark's own scorer, the same model with and without Morpheus on the same tasks, pass rules written down before the run. Group travel: MemoryArena (arXiv 2602.16313). Both November 15, 2026; internal runs, not reviewed by a third party.

Where it goes: from checking one plan, to one action (a purchase, a command), to one experiment, to an organization's decisions. What a checker cannot see goes to the step that proposes new hypotheses and runs experiments that tell them apart; Learning to Investigate measures how well LLM agents do that step today. Where it came from: a planning engine for science (Morphism-Oriented Reasoning with Provable, Honest, Epistemic Understanding for Science) that builds a knowledge graph from papers — 18,600+ scientific steps from 665 documents, every one traced to its page — plans over it, and accepts a plan only after a checker passes it. Research: EMNLP Findings 2025 and four 2026 preprints. Honeyguide (formerly Rung) · for teams paying for LLM APIs

See the saving and the quality cost before you switch to a cheaper model.

A one-day test that runs alongside your live traffic. All day, your users get the top model's answers (in the hosted trial, DeepSeek's pro-max, on our key and at our cost). Behind each request, Honeyguide records what a cheap-model-first setup would have answered and what it would have cost. You mark about 30 answers, and the report says whether a cheaper setup is enough for your traffic.

  • Cheap model first, checked. The cheap model answers first, a check decides what goes out, and a failed answer climbs to a stronger model, under a hard cap per request. Our test: 76% cheaper than always using the top model, for 8 points of quality ($1.86 against $7.75 per 1,000 requests; 69% against 77% right).
  • The report picks the setup. Sometimes the answer is one cheaper model, not a chain of models. On the same test one cheaper model got 73% right at $1.43 per 1,000 requests — better than the chain on both counts.
  • One URL to change. It uses the OpenAI API format: point your client at it for the day.

Try it: honeyguide-trial.onrender.com. The trial is invite-only; applications are open, and no team has run a test day yet. Honeyguide is open source: the code is published when the first team applies.

Our test: 100 short RouterArena questions on DeepSeek models, September 2026, not reviewed by anyone outside. Write-ups: Can a cheaper model do your work? · Don't predict, verify (as Rung) · related work: routers and cascades.

Trace Check-up · for teams running AI agents

Post-train your agent on the logs you already have. Step one: a check-up.

Large companies post-train their agents with training platforms that do the work around the training: picking the data from their logs, writing the success check, and checking the trained model. Trace Check-up gives any team that workflow, with its own logs, its own success check and its own test tasks, on its own training-platform account. You train; we never do. In order: what to record, a check-up of the logs you have (today), the data ready for your platform, and a check of the trained model on tasks it did not train on, at the same cost (coming soon).

The check-up reads your agent's log on your own computer. In about a minute it says what the log is missing, whether there is enough of it, and how training compares with letting the agent try again — or that it can't tell yet, and what to collect.

  • Train, or try again? Two cards side by side: what trying again does, measured on your own log, and a rough range for training a small model, with the published results it is based on. Either card can say “can't tell yet”.
  • Do you have enough? Tasks tried at least 8 times, conversations that worked and who decided they worked, tasks that never worked — and how many more to collect.
  • What to record. The eleven things a log needs before it can answer, and the ones yours is missing.
  • Honest by construction. Every sentence points to a reading of your log or a cited paper, and it says “can't tell” when the log cannot decide. It never promises that training will help, and no training platform pays us, so the advice does not depend on which way you go.

Status: a small tool that runs on your own computer, so your log never leaves it. It is open source: the code is published when the first team asks for it; until then the example report (on a made-up log) shows what it makes. Getting your data ready for your training platform is coming soon. Comparing a trained agent with the untrained one at the same cost is coming soon. Product page.
