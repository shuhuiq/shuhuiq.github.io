Can a cheaper model do your work? Measure it before you switch

October 2026

Many teams send every request to the top model. Nobody wants to be the person who switched to a cheaper model and broke something. But a cheaper model may be good enough for most of the traffic. The real question is how to know before you switch.

I started on this question with a model router, Rung. Two findings changed what I build.

  • Predicting the model did not work. Reading the prompt to predict which model is enough recovered none of the available savings in our tests, across every set of features we tried.
  • Routing became free. In August 2026 several large cloud platforms began to offer model routing as a free feature. A router alone is no longer something a team needs to buy.

So the check and the report became the product, not the router. It is now called Honeyguide.

A shadow day

  • Change one URL. Point your OpenAI-format client at Honeyguide for one day. Your users keep your current model's answers; nothing changes for them.
  • Behind each request, Honeyguide also lets the cheap model answer first. A check decides if that answer is good enough, and a failed answer climbs to a stronger model, under a hard cap per request. Honeyguide records what this setup would have answered and what it would have cost.
  • You mark about 30 answers right or wrong. The report then says whether a cheaper setup is enough for your traffic. Sometimes the answer is one cheaper model, not a cascade.

What our test showed

Our test used 100 short questions from RouterArena on a ladder of DeepSeek models, on 26 September 2026.

SetupRight$ per 1,000 requests
Top model for everything (pro-max)77%7.75
Honeyguide cascade69%1.86
One cheaper model for everything (pro-low)73%1.43
Cheapest model for everything (chat)66%0.20

The cascade cost 76% less than the top model, for 8 points of quality. But on this traffic one cheaper model did better than the cascade on both counts: more answers right, and a lower bill. That is exactly the kind of answer the report exists to give.

Three surprises

  1. A check can cost more than a cheap answer. One verdict cost $0.00055; one answer from the cheapest model cost $0.00019. A cascade saves money only when the check is cheap next to the answers it saves you from buying.
  2. Embeddings cannot judge answers. We asked whether an embedding puts “the code is correct” nearer to “the code is incorrect” or to “the implementation is right”. Both encoders we tried, text-embedding-3-large too, put the opposite nearer. Raw embedding similarity cannot tell a right answer from a wrong one.
  3. Weak judges mislead. Our rule now: the judge must be at least as strong as the model it checks.

Status

The trial is live and invite-only: apply at honeyguide-trial.onrender.com. Applications are open, and no team has run a shadow day yet. Honeyguide is open source: the code is published when the first team applies.

Our test: 100 questions, not reviewed by anyone outside. The set may be easy, because a cheap model came close to the top one. The DeepSeek models behind these numbers have since been renamed or retired, so the numbers may not hold on the current models. The pro-low row is a separate run, with replies capped at 700 tokens.
