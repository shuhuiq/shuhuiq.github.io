my story

Most of my work comes back to one question: when can a system act on the signal it is given? In my PhD I trained multi-agent reinforcement learning policies to schedule large stochastic production networks, and at Samsung Display I shipped one — a scheduler for lines with hundreds of machines, where a reward or a constraint that is slightly wrong costs real money. Six of my patent applications are about exactly this: reweighting rewards, adapting a policy to a new reward, and letting a safety policy step in.

With LLM agents the question comes back in a harder form. Where a simulator or a design-rule checker can score the outcome, as in the chip-design environments my team builds, the reward can be trusted. Most agent tasks can only be partly checked, and the usual answer is to ask another model. In my own measurements, the LLM judges that agreed most with human labels still accepted plans that broke the user's budget — a shortcut an RL policy could learn to exploit. So I build checkers that do not just say “looks good”. They point to the line and the amount, and say “unclear” instead of guessing. Morpheus gives every AI conclusion and every AI action a receipt you can check. What a checker cannot see, such as a variable the question left out, needs new hypotheses and experiments that tell explanations apart; in Learning to Investigate I measured how well LLM research agents do that step. The same question about money led to Honeyguide, which measures what a cheaper model setup would save and lose before a team switches, and to Trace Check-up, which helps any team post-train its agent on the logs it already has.

The other thing I have learned is that a number is only as good as the way it was measured. I freeze held-out sets, write down what counts as a result before the run, measure once, and report the runs that did not work.

RL that ships

Multi-agent RL scheduling in production: hundreds of machines, tens of thousands of orders a day, 20% lower operating cost. Patent applications on reward design and safe RL.

I am a Senior Staff ML Engineer and Senior Engineering Manager at Samsung Display American Lab in San Jose, and the project lead for three programs: LLM agents for display-chip design, AI-driven materials discovery, and multi-agent reinforcement learning (RL) for production scheduling. I lead a team of 8 researchers and engineers and own delivery to the design, materials and manufacturing teams.

  • Chip design. I built ChipEnv, a tool-use environment over the circuit and layout stack (circuit simulation, design-rule checking, parasitic RC extraction). Fine-tuning on agent trajectories taught LLMs to use the design tools, and RL with verifiable rewards from the design-rule checker and RC extraction taught them better layouts. The agent designs high-scoring layouts close to human experts' in one to two days.
  • Materials. I lead the search for long-lifetime deep-blue TADF emitters for OLED displays: constrained molecular generation, property predictors, molecular-dynamics simulation, RL-based candidate search and retrosynthesis filtering.
  • Scheduling. Our multi-agent RL scheduler runs on production lines with hundreds of machines and tens of thousands of orders a day, and cut operating cost by 20% against the previous scheduler.
  • Earlier. ChatYield, an LLM agent for yield optimization (fine-tuned Llama-3; every proposed action is tested in a digital-twin simulation before an engineer sees it), and production ML for predictive maintenance (100% recall at a 48-hour lead time) and defect inspection (99% recall, 95% of inspections automated).
2019 -

As an affiliated researcher at Stanford, I build Morpheus (morpheusplan.com): a receipt you can check, for every AI conclusion and every AI action. Each of the user's requirements gets one of three verdicts: met, with the arithmetic, quote or execution result behind it; violated, with the line and the amount, repaired in place and checked again; or unclear, with what is missing and who has to supply it. With the same model, Morpheus took trip planning from 56% to 98% of 296 unseen tasks with no false “met”, and group travel from 4.4% to 84.8%. The same checker stops a coding agent's rule-breaking command before it runs, inside Claude Code, Codex and Cursor, and keeps experiment records that others can recompute. Morpheus grew out of a planning engine for science that builds a knowledge graph from papers (18,600+ actions from 665 documents, each traced to its page) and accepts a plan only after a verifier passes it. The research behind it: EMNLP Findings 2025 and four 2026 preprints. It is in an invite-only beta.

2026

I also build Honeyguide (formerly Rung; trial), a one-day shadow test for teams that pay for LLM APIs. It shows what a cheaper model setup would save, and what it would lose in quality, on the team's own traffic before they switch.

2026

And Trace Check-up. Large companies post-train their agents with training platforms that do the work around the training: picking the data from their logs, writing the success check, and checking the trained model. Trace Check-up gives any team that workflow, with its own logs, its own success check and its own held-out tasks, on its own training-platform account. Today it reads the logs on the team's own computer and says what to record, what is missing and how much more to collect; getting the data ready for the platform and checking the trained model are coming soon.

2026

On scientific discovery, I designed Learning to Investigate, a pre-registered benchmark that measures where LLM research agents fall short: choosing experiments, noticing that the model they were given is wrong, and proposing a better one. Each world hides a mechanism over six inputs, and the agent gets ten noisy experiments, with strong statistical baselines on the same data. Lessons from 60 past episodes transferred to new mechanisms no better than lessons from 5; one sentence of procedural advice cost about one nat of information; and the agent named all three variables of a three-way interaction in 45% of episodes but wrote the interaction in 15%. These gaps set the next targets: experiments that separate the explanations still in play, and lessons that carry a tested scope.

bio

Shuhui Qu is a machine learning researcher and engineer working on reinforcement learning and on AI agents whose answers can be checked. At Samsung Display American Lab, Shuhui is a Senior Staff ML Engineer and the project lead for chip-design agents, materials discovery and multi-agent RL scheduling; at Stanford, Shuhui builds Morpheus (morpheusplan.com), which gives every AI conclusion and action a receipt you can check, and two smaller tools: Honeyguide, which measures what a cheaper LLM setup would save and lose before a team switches, and Trace Check-up, which helps any team post-train its agent on the logs it already has. Shuhui also studies where LLM research agents fall short (Learning to Investigate). PhD, Stanford University. 50+ papers, 1,700+ citations, 10 granted US patents and more pending.
